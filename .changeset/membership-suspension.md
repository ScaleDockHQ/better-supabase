Memberships can be suspended. A `disabledAt` column on the `tenant` module's memberships table (`disabled_at`, added to managed tables; an adopted table opts in through `sql.modules.tenant.columns.memberships.disabledAt`) keeps the member's role but takes away their permissions in that tenant: `member_can`, `tenant_ids_with`, `member_permissions`, `can_assign_as`, `permission_claims`, `member_organization_ids`, `has_organization_role` and `membership_claims` skip a suspended membership, and the `provider` and `custom` models refuse it too. The organizations module adds `suspend_member` and `resume_member` (`organizations.suspendMember` and `resumeMember` in the block, with the `organization.member_suspended` and `organization.member_resumed` events and audit entries), which refuse the caller's own membership, the last active owner and members above the caller. A suspended owner no longer counts for the owner check, `transfer_ownership` refuses a suspended new owner (`ORGANIZATION_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`), the role guard treats a client write to `disabled_at` like a role change, and `list_members` and `list_my_organizations` return `disabled_at` (`disabledAt` in the block). The two list functions are dropped and recreated, so `sql sync` also rewrites their `api` entry points.
